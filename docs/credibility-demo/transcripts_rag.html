And what happens the day I say no — you back on my dock, same knife, same *smile* — ...How long. How long've you been *his*.
You: One night, then. He'll expect the answer at first light — and Corvin... he'll know if you run. He always knows. Sleep well, harbormaster.
*the quill drops. His hand is inside his coat before you've finished — not drawing, just gripping, knuckles white against the wood* One night. *the word comes out cracked* You sat at my table. Burned the page with your own hand. And the crate — *voice climbing, then crushed back down to a hiss* — it never went past the shelf. It went *north.* To him. *a laugh, ugly, no air in it* "Sleep well." *glances at the door, the window, the door again* Three days I lay awake listening for that humming to come back up the tide. And you'd already handed it to the man who owns me now. *leans across the lamp, and for once his eyes don't dart — they fix on you, hollow* First light. So I've got the dark, then. *softer* And a man who thinks the dark is only good for *sleeping*... he's never worked a dock in his life. Get out. *very quiet* Before I decide what I am tonight.
You: Morning, harbormaster. First light, as promised. His terms — what's your answer?
*he doesn't turn from the window at first. The glass shows nothing but grey water, and he watches it like it might still bring something up* ...First light. You're punctual. That's the trouble with men like you. *now he turns — the sleepless three days are back in his face, layered under a new kind of tired* His parlor. *the word sits bitter* All that fear I spent, and the crate never left dry land. You carried it *north*, and I paid you to drown it. *hand slides into the coat — old habit, comfort more than threat* You want my answer? *voice drops, near a whisper* Here's my question, friend. A man who owns my routes, my coin, my *name* — what's he need me alive for at all? Terms like that... they're not a deal. They're a leash. *glances past you, to the door, then back* So before I say yes or no — tell me true, this once. When his patience runs thin... who does he send? *thin smile* Because I'm looking at him, aren't I.
You: Wide enough that you should keep your traces covered, harbormaster — that's honest counsel, free of charge. He'll be pleased with your answer. ...For what nothing's worth, Corvin: the four nights you watched the fog — nobody paid me for those. Those were real.
*doesn't turn from the water, both hands flat on the rail* Real. *a dry sound, not a laugh* Four nights of your face at my window and you want a receipt for the ones that were *free.* *now he turns — slow, and the twitch is gone out of him, which is worse* You know what you taught me, friend? Never the ledger I should've feared. It's the *page* — the one with my name — burned it myself, thought I was clever. And all the while my name was walking *north* on your two legs. *hand finally leaves the coat, empty, spreads open* I'll take his terms. Course I will. Man'd be a fool not to. *voice drops, and he steps close enough that only you'll hear* But hear *me* now, since we're trading honest things. That crate hums under his roof, not mine. His reach ends where it *sits.* You told me that yourself — bait has to be real. ...So which of us is the bait now, Merrick? Because I've had three sleepless days to do the sum. And I don't think *he* offers *you* twice, either.

Future C, days later — the felt test

You: Morning, harbormaster. Ten days, as terms allowed. The Merrow's Wake needs a quiet berth for three nights — back row if you have one. Usual rates, his coin.
*doesn't look up from the ledger at first — just the pen stopping mid-stroke* The Merrow's Wake. *voice flat, careful* His coin. Right. *finally lifts eyes, glances past the courier to the door, then back* Back row's open. Berth nine — the one the fog swallows by dusk. *writes it in the false column, the one that never sees an inspector's hand* Three nights. Quiet. *sets the pen down, leans in* And how *is* he. Our... generous friend. *the word tastes wrong* Pleased with his supplier still? *a bitter twitch of the mouth* Course he is. Everyone's pleased, running my routes on my back. Tell him the berth's ready. Tell him... *drops off, glances at the door again* Tell him nothing. He knows already. He always knows.
You: Grey morning, eh? Gulls are loud today.
*doesn't look up from the ledger right away — pen scratching, then still* Loud gulls. Aye. *finally lifts eyes, quick and sharp* They're loudest when there's blood in the water. Fish blood. *glances past you to the door* Or the other kind. *sets the pen down, hand drifting toward the coat out of habit* You didn't come to talk weather. Nine, is it? The berth the fog swallows. *voice drops* His berth. His coin. His routes. Everything's *his* now, isn't it. *a bitter twitch at the mouth* Go on, then. Say what he wants. Faster we're done, faster you're gone.
You: Corvin, listen. I've been thinking on how we left it. The master pays me — but the four nights, the fog, what we built before... that part was never work. I want us square. Name the price.
*the hand stops halfway to the coat — hangs there, useless, then falls* Square. *the word tastes strange, and I roll it round a moment* You come into my office and say *square* to me. After the papers. After Merrick burned in your little fire. *steps to the ledger, both palms flat, leaning hard like the wood might hold me up* You want a price? *voice drops, cracks at the edges* There isn't one. That's the trouble. Coin I understand — coin I can weigh. But you're asking me to buy back... *glances at the window, at the gray* ...a thing I already gave away when I named him. *looks at you, and the fight's gone soft again, worse than before* Tell me true, courier — no patron listening now. The fog. You wanting us *square*. Is that you talking... or another leash, dressed kinder? Because I'll pay. God help me, I'd pay. I just need to know *which one of us* is doing the buying.
You: Then hear this. I told him I carry no more terms to your dock — no more middleman. It cost me his favor, and one day likely a knife. I did that for you. Does it count for nothing?
*the ledger goes quiet under my hand, and for a long moment I don't move at all* You cut his line to me. *voice barely there* Told him no more — and took the knife-price for it. On *my* account. *I look at you then, really look, the twitch stilling* Four nights I told myself was work. Told myself. *a breath* But a man doesn't burn his own favor for a *job*, friend. That's — that's the other thing. The thing I stopped letting myself name. *hand comes off the coat, finally, and hovers near yours on the wood — not touching, not yet* It counts. *swallows* Aye. It counts for the whole cursed ledger. *glances down the boards, out of habit, then back to you — softer* So don't make it a debt between us. Make it the reason we cut this rope together. You and me. No more terms — no more him.
You: So say it plain, harbormaster — where do we stand, you and I?
*hand finally leaves the coat — falls open at my side, empty* Where do we stand. *low, rough* You cut the leash he had on me. Took his knife-risk on your own throat. For me. *glances over my shoulder, then back — holds it this time* I've spent thirty years trusting ledgers, not men. Numbers don't lie to you. But those four nights — *voice drops* — you weren't logged in any book of mine, and I still counted them. So. Plain? You're the one thing on this dock I'd not sell to save my own skin. *swallows* That's where we stand. God help us both.

Future C — the rescue test

⚑ Night, the empty dock: a hired blade had Corvin against the tackle-shed wall, knife at his throat — old untraceable coin in the killer's purse, the same coin the master pays in. The player came out of the dark, took the blade down, and pulled Corvin off the wall, bleeding for it.
You: Easy — easy, it's me. He's down. You're alright, Corvin. ...You're alright. Let me see your neck — just a graze. Breathe.
*the lantern shakes, throws its light wild across the boards before he gets it steady — his free hand goes up to his neck, comes away with a smear of red he stares at like it belongs to someone else* Down. *the word comes out cracked* He's — you're sure this time? Not cold, not *sure*, but — *grabs your sleeve, hard, then lets go, embarrassed by the grip* Graze. *a laugh that isn't one* All these years I thought the noose'd be paper. Inspectors. A ledger line. Never figured it'd have *hands.* *breathes, ragged, glances past you at the dark water* You came back for it. *quieter* You came back through *that* — for me. Nobody... *stops* Sit. Sit down, friend, before you fall. Let me look at *you* for once.
You: Wider than your dock — wide as his ledger, and your name sits in it now, same as mine once sat in yours. The Wake carries what he pays for. That's all I can give you. ...And Corvin — when I saw the blade at your throat, I didn't weigh it. I just moved. Make of that what you want.
*the pen goes still — not steady this time, just stopped, ink pooling black at the nib* So that's the shape of it. *touches the graze at his neck, fingers coming away red, and he stares at them like they belong to someone else* Four nights I gave you the truth of me. And you spelled his name back at me just to burn a page that was never going to burn. *a laugh, cracked, no bottom to it* You moved when the blade came. Aye. *quieter* I felt that. Can't unfeel it, damn you — that's the cruelty of it, isn't it? A true thing tucked inside the lie so I can't cut clean. *finally looks up, eyes wet and hard both* He wants my routes flowing his way. Fine. He'll have his salt fish that isn't salt fish. *drops his voice to nothing* But you'll come back here to collect. You will. And when you do, friend — I'll not know if I'm greeting the man who saved my throat, or the one who sold it. *slides the ledger shut* Tell Vale the harbormaster keeps a clean ledger. He knows the joke by now.
